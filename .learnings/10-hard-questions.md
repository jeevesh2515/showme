# 10 Hard questions

Purpose: rehearse the questions a skeptical judge, investor or interviewer would ask, with answers that hold up when pushed. Tags are explained in [README.md](README.md). The rule for every answer here: admit the weakness first, then say what is measured, what is planned and what would change my mind. As of 2026-10-27 only spikes and docs exist, and I say so whenever it matters.

### Q: Isn't this just a wrapper around an API?

**Short answer:** [Decided] The model call is one part. The work that is mine is the client-side redaction with a mandatory review and a type that blocks raw images, the honesty policy around the model's output, the SAM agreement gate, the rate-limit and spend-cap layer, and a reproducible evaluation harness that runs the production code.

**Detail:**
- Two Meta models do the vision. I do not claim to have trained anything.
- Examples of logic that is not in the API: the post-processing and honesty gate, the verifier plan for a measured calibration gap, and the decision not to move the click based on SAM after measuring that refinement did not help.
- Most of the value I am claiming is in evidence and engineering discipline, not in a new model. If a judge wants novelty in the model, I do not have it.

**Evidence:** [ARCHITECTURE.md sections 5 to 9 and 13](../docs/ARCHITECTURE.md), [SPIKES.md summary](../docs/SPIKES.md)

### Q: Your benchmark is easy, so what?

**Short answer:** [Known limitation] The one I have run is easy. ScreenSpot v1 with 10 samples is near-saturated and only shows the API works. The real tests are ScreenSpot-v2 in full, ScreenSpot-Pro stratified to 300, a 200-sample negatives suite and a 20-task end-to-end suite, and I have written kill criteria for a bad result.

**Detail:**
- 10/10 and 15/16 are smoke tests. n is tiny.
- Pro was chosen because it has headroom; the target is only at least 40% and the pivot trigger is below 25%.
- Nothing is published until it is generated by the harness. Until then the right number is "not run".

**Evidence:** [SPIKES.md (b)](../docs/SPIKES.md), [RISKS.md R1](../docs/RISKS.md), [RULES.md section 2](../docs/RULES.md)

### Q: Why should anyone trust the redaction?

**Short answer:** [Known limitation] They should not trust it alone, and the product does not ask them to. It catches patterned data (10/10, 0 false positives on synthetic fixtures), but misses free text such as names and addresses, so the user must check a mandatory review screen.

**Detail:**
- The input-field heuristic scored 7/11 with 6 false positives and 0/3 in dark mode, so it is not in the MVP.
- Fixtures are synthetic and clean, so real recall will be lower.
- The type system and a network test are designed to make sure that nothing but the reviewed image is sent. That protects against bugs. It does not protect against a user who misses a name on the review screen.

**Evidence:** [SPIKES.md (d)](../docs/SPIKES.md), [RISKS.md R3](../docs/RISKS.md), [REQUIREMENTS.md PRIV-1, PRIV-2](../docs/REQUIREMENTS.md)

### Q: SAM did not help, so why keep it?

**Short answer:** [Measured] SAM does not improve click accuracy, and on ScreenSpot boxes the model's own box scored higher: IoU 0.57 against 0.51 for SAM with a box prompt and 0.41 with a point. I keep it as a gated highlight tool because it is a required Meta model and it beat the model box on 3 of 5 hits on clean synthetic elements, and I will report the ablation as it comes out.

**Detail:**
- The outline appears only when it agrees with the model box. It can never move the click.
- ScreenSpot's ground-truth boxes are often loose regions, which penalises a tight mask. So IoU there is a weak proxy, and human ratings on the E2E suite decide.
- If human ratings show no benefit, I keep SAM only where it wins and say so (R5).

**Evidence:** [SPIKES.md (c)](../docs/SPIKES.md), [RISKS.md R5](../docs/RISKS.md), [MEMORY.md D3, D4, A8](../docs/MEMORY.md)

### Q: What if Meta changes the model?

**Short answer:** [Decided] I pin the model ID `muse-spark-1.3`, log the model in every result, and keep a prompt-version and cache design so any change shows up as a new run. I have no second vendor, so a withdrawal would hurt.

**Detail:**
- If a model is updated in place, results could shift. Reproduction depends on the model staying put.
- Fallbacks inside the Meta family: Muse Wonder for grounding. For SAM: local SAM 2.1 tiny for eval only.
- Wonder is a preview model, so it carries more change risk than Spark.

**Evidence:** [RISKS.md watch list](../docs/RISKS.md), [ARCHITECTURE.md section 6](../docs/ARCHITECTURE.md), [SPIKES.md (a)](../docs/SPIKES.md)

**Detail:**

### Q: The model's confidence is useless. How can you claim honesty?

**Short answer:** [Measured] I do not rely on confidence. The spike's `target_visible` flag was right on 5/5 absent-target probes, and the plan adds a verifier pass if confidence AUROC is below 0.65. If that does not lift precision, I drop confidence from the UI and mark every step "Check before you tap".

**Detail:**
- Honest nuance: the 5/5 used the spike's own prompt and schema, not production prompt v1. I will re-measure on the 200-sample negatives suite.
- All ten hits were at 0.95 to 0.98, so I have no misses to compare against yet. The finding is that confidence is uniformly high, not a calibration curve.
- "Never a silent guess" is a UX rule enforced by the `unsure` state, not a claim of perfection.

**Evidence:** [SPIKES.md summary and (b)](../docs/SPIKES.md), [ARCHITECTURE.md section 5.5](../docs/ARCHITECTURE.md), [RISKS.md R2](../docs/RISKS.md)

### Q: Why do you quote 10/10 when n is 10?

### Q: What if a wrong step makes someone cancel the wrong thing, or pay?

**Short answer:** [Known limitation] That risk is real. ShowMe never clicks for the user, shows a clear highlight and one sentence, adds cautions on payments, codes and gift cards, and marks unsure steps. It cannot guarantee correctness.

**Detail:**
- The calibration gap means a wrong step can look certain. That is why the verifier plan and the "check before you tap" framing exist.
- Caution is triggered for steps involving paying, sharing a code or password, remote-access apps, gift cards, or suspicious messages.
- The README Limitations section will say this directly. I have not measured how often a wrong step happens.

**Evidence:** [ARCHITECTURE.md sections 5.2 and 5.5](../docs/ARCHITECTURE.md), [DESIGN.md section 4.4](../docs/DESIGN.md), [PRD.md section 5.3](../docs/PRD.md)

### Q: Realistically, will a 77-year-old take a screenshot, upload it and review a privacy screen?

**Detail:**

### Q: What stops judges from draining your credit?

**Short answer:** [Decided] Per-IP limits of 30 steps an hour and 150 a day, and a global cap of $5 a day and $60 total. If total spend hits $110, anonymous use switches to a demo mode with sample screens and 10 live steps per IP per day.

**Detail:**

### Q: What if Vercel's limits break the backend?

**Short answer:** [Known limitation] The limits are UNVERIFIED for Python functions: the 4.5 MB body cap and the duration limit against my timeouts. I deploy on Day 4 specifically to find out, and the same Docker image can move to Render or Cloud Run the same day.

**Detail:**

### Q: Doesn't redaction blind the model and hurt accuracy?

**Short answer:** [Known limitation] It may, and I do not know by how much. The public benchmarks run without redaction because those images contain no user PII, so only the E2E suite, which runs the real redaction pipeline, will show the effect.

**Detail:**

### Q: Why are you using two calls? Isn't 4 to 5 seconds too slow?

**Short answer:** [Measured] The box appears after the grounding call (p50 2,809 ms at minimal effort) instead of also waiting for SAM (p50 1,338 ms more), which is D5's "about 1.3 s sooner". The outline follows when SAM returns. Whether that feels fast enough to a nervous user is untested, and my target is p50 at most 3.5 s and p95 at most 7 s server side.

**Detail:**
- Run in sequence, grounding plus SAM is about 4 to 5 s (RISKS R4). The two-call design is how the user sees the box without waiting for that total. Production uses one SAM call.
- Progress text changes live ("Looking at your screen", "Finding the button", "Drawing the outline"), with a "taking longer" message after 10 seconds.
- Pivot: if production p95 is above 8 s, use smaller images; if SAM p95 is above 4 s, make the outline opt-in.

**Evidence:** [SPIKES.md (b) and (c)](../docs/SPIKES.md), [RISKS.md R4](../docs/RISKS.md), [DESIGN.md section 4.3](../docs/DESIGN.md)
