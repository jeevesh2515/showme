# Three directions

Director's draft, 4 November 2026. Reference research and challenge are added below by the story researcher. Source times are in the phone recording (`incoming/phone-20261104/recipient-original.mov`) or the selected desktop take (`out/raw/main`, seconds from first frame).

## A. "The point that doesn't move" (chosen)

**Premise.** Every shot in the film is registered so the same real button sits on the same spot of the frame: everything around it changes (original, masked, uploaded, answered, outlined, phoned), and the point holds still. This belongs to ShowMe because its spoken engineering promise is literally "the click point never moves", and its product idea is one point on a screen.

**First five seconds.**
- Frame 0: the unmarked synthetic billing screen fills the frame, dense and small. Upper half: "Where do I press?" in very large type on ink. Two yellow marks in the frame's edge bands are hunting along their rulers.
- 0–3.9s: voice says the line. Slow push in, scaling about the fixed point. Marks keep hunting.
- 3.9s: hard cut. Plate gone. Same button, same place, now with the recorded ring and badge from the actual guide. Marks lock on it.
- 3.9–5s: word "There." sits beside it, in the empty space, then settles. Label: real output, iPhone 16 self-demo.

**Signature device.** Registration marks: two yellow notches on the frame's own ink rulers, outside the footage. Evolution: hunt then lock (hook) / lock with the real values x 482, y 632 (Spark) / hold perfectly still across the outline crossfade (SAM) / lock through phone and original (handoff) / hunt and never lock, then fold away (not visible) / lock again on the opening frame and become the URL's underline (close).

**Motion law.** The camera only ever scales about the fixed point. So every move, including the founder's real pinch stabilised on the highlight, converges on the same place.

**Source moments.**
1. Phone 16.8s (pinched guide, recorded ring) cut to phone 28.7s (original in Photos): the button lands within about 40 native pixels at the same size.
2. Desktop 22.31s onward: real box-to-outline crossfade, then masks in `uploaded.jpg` at the same registration.

**Risk.** Credibility: a registered cut could read as one continuous action, so each state change needs a source label. Execution: registration data must be measured per source and desktop frames get soft past about 2.2x. Comprehension: the device is subtle, so it must be carried by the cuts, not the notches alone.

## B. "From where to there" (editorial thread)

**Premise.** A yellow editorial thread runs from the question, through the reviewed image, to the guide and the original control, then underlines the CTA.

**First five seconds.**
- Frame 0: billing screen with the question at its top left.
- 0–2s: thread starts at the question mark, moves right and down outside the screen edge.
- 2–4s: thread reaches the frame edge beside the button and stops.
- 4–5s: cut to the guide with the recorded ring; thread continues in the margin.

**Signature device.** The thread travels between shots and becomes section dividers and the URL underline.

**Source moments.** Desktop 22.31s (ring appears); phone 26.2–29.6s (original in Photos).

**Risk.** Visual overload and genericness: any product could use a line. It decorates the story without proving anything, needs a travelling element in every shot, and must stay off the footage, which forces inset footage and shrinks the main material.

## C. "What left the browser" (privacy reveal)

**Premise.** Open on the masks: a wall of black bars, then wipe to reveal the same screen with its private data, showing what never left. Privacy review as the hook rather than the problem.

**First five seconds.**
- Frame 0: `uploaded.jpg` full frame, mostly black bars and a scrim.
- 0–2s: voice says the screenshot line; bars read as mysterious.
- 2–4s: wipe across to the unmasked original.
- 4–5s: the ring on the masked version.

**Signature device.** A vertical wipe seam that compares before and after, later used for box against outline and for guide against original.

**Source moments.** Desktop 5.18–16.5s (masks and manual corrections); `uploaded.jpg` against `billing.png`.

**Risk.** Confusion: a cold viewer sees redaction before knowing the problem, and the voice is saying something else. The opening also displays synthetic personal data as the reveal, which can read as exposure. Strong as a mid-film moment; wrong as the film's spine. Its wipe idea moves into direction A for the privacy payoff.

## Decision

**A.** It is the only one whose device is also a claim about the product, gives the first payoff in under four seconds from real footage, and makes the best real moment (the phone guide to original cut) the film's natural climax. It needs no decoration, and the footage can run close to full frame. B's useful element (a line ending as the URL underline) and C's useful element (the before/after wipe) are absorbed where they serve A.

## Researcher challenge

Added 4 November 2026 by the story researcher. This is an opinion from reading the text of the concept, not from watching a render. No viewer has been tested. It makes no claim about reach or outcomes.

**First five seconds, cold viewer, sound off.**
- 0 to 3.9s: "Where do I press?" is a clear question, and a dense billing screen is a recognisable cause. That part works. The yellow marks hunting on rulers read as decoration or a loading glitch to someone who does not know the idea. They carry nothing yet.
- 3.9s: the hard cut to a ring on the button is the real payoff, and it answers the question. But the question is on screen only if the type stays up through the cut; if it vanishes with the plate, a muted viewer sees "button gets a circle" with no stated cause.
- 3.9 to 5s: "There." is the right size of answer. It does not say what produced the ring. A cold viewer will guess "an app drew a circle", which is true and not yet specific. Nothing in the first five seconds says screenshot, goal or private data.

**Is it specific or a gimmick?** The claim (the point never moves) is specific to ShowMe and checkable, but it is an engineering promise, and a cold viewer does not yet know why a point would move. Registration marks only become meaningful after the viewer has seen two states of the same point. Until the SAM and handoff beats, the notches are a style choice. The risk is that the device is more legible to you than to the audience.

**What I would change.**
1. Keep the question on screen across the 3.9s cut, and add a muted-safe verb line at 0 to 3.9s, for example "Screenshot + goal" in small type under the question, so the cut shows input then output.
2. At 3.9 to 5s, add one source label tied to the real clip: "Real output, iPhone 16". The concept already has this; keep it on from the first frame of the ring, not after it settles.
3. Do not spend the first four seconds on hunting marks. Either start them locked on the right button from 0s as a quiet constant (so the audience learns the point first), or drop them until the second shot where the point survives a change. Hunting then locking only works if hunting is visibly a search for something; an unexplained yellow wobble is not.
4. Say the privacy and review step early enough in the cold open that the audience is not left thinking a raw screenshot is being uploaded: by about 8 to 10s, not later.
5. Check feed size: the ring at 3.9s must be at least as large as the question text was at 0s, or the muted payoff reads weaker than the setup.

**Verdict on the chosen direction.** Keep A. The 3.9s cut is the strongest idea in it, but the first 3.9s are carried only by voice, and the hunting marks add nothing for a muted viewer. Fix those two points before building.
