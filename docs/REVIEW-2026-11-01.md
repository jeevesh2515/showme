# ShowMe: honest review and plan to 8.5+

Date: 2026-11-01 (Day 7, feature freeze Mon Nov 2 23:59 London). Reviewer stance: senior engineer and hackathon judge. Every number below comes from a generated file in `eval/results/` or from a measured run in this session; anything else is marked as an estimate or UNVERIFIED.

## 1. Verdict in one paragraph

ShowMe is an unusually well-engineered hackathon project with a real idea at its core, but the story around the engineering is weaker than the engineering. The build is strong: a live app, client-side redaction that never uploads the raw picture, a SAM agreement gate, 49 browser tests, a public eval harness with 11 result rows in `RESULTS.md`, weak numbers included. What holds it back is evidence about people. No usability session or outside user is recorded in the repo, the primary persona (a 77-year-old doing a screenshot-upload-review loop four times per task) is the person least able to operate it, the "honest" principle misses its own target by 28 points, and the published accuracy numbers come from a prompt version that is no longer the one in production. All four are fixable before submission.

**Current score: 7.2 / 10** (mean of the six dimensions below: (7.0 + 8.5 + 7.5 + 6.5 + 6.0 + 7.5) / 6 = 7.17). Path to 8.5 in section 8.

## 3. Holes in the thinking

Ranked by how much a sharp judge would hurt you with them.

### 3.1 The primary user cannot run the loop

The PRD's primary persona is Ken, 77, low patience for jargon. To get one step he must: take a screenshot (two-button chord), switch to a browser, find ShowMe, add the picture, read and approve a redaction review, read the step, switch back, act, then repeat for every step (3.7 steps on average in E2E). Estimate (not measured): screenshot 1 + switch to browser 1 + tap "Choose a picture" 1 + pick the photo 1 + approve review 1 + switch back 1 + act 1 + return to ShowMe 1 = 8 actions per step; × 3.7 average steps (`eval/results/2026-11-01-e2e-final/report.md`) ≈ 30 actions per task, on a phone, for the least confident user in the market.

The person who will actually operate ShowMe is **Priya, the helper relative**. She already receives screenshots from her parent on WhatsApp today. ShowMe lets her turn those screenshots into a highlighted, private, expiring guide in one minute instead of a 20-minute phone call. That makes the Share feature the core of the product, not an optional extra after "done".

**Change:** reposition the primary user as the helper, and the stuck person as the recipient of a guide. Little code, big story change (section 7, item 2).

### 3.2 "Honest over clever" is the principle you measure worst

PRD §6.1 target: ≥ 90% honest answers when the target is not on screen. Measured on held-out negatives: **131 / 210 = 62.4%** said `not_visible`; refusals were 12 / 12. So on more than a third of screens where the button does not exist, ShowMe highlights something anyway. For a user worried about "pressing the wrong thing", this is the most dangerous failure the product has, and it is the principle the landing page leads with.

The README line "Correctly stated not_visible, unsure, or refuse: 64.4%" is accurate but blends the two, which a judge will read as hiding the miss. The R2 pivot (drop confidence from the UI, D23) was executed, but no pivot exists for honesty itself.

### 3.3 Published numbers are not from the shipped build

All held-out runs (v2 90.7%, Pro 63.7%, negatives 64.4%) are prompt **v1** (`pvv1` in the result folder names). Production runs prompt **v2** (`backend/showme/core/prompts.py:3`), validated on dev-100 only (92.0% vs 93.0%). PHASES Day 7 "final eval re-run with the frozen prompt" is unticked. A judge who reads folder names will notice.

### 3.4 E2E 70% has no unseen task set behind it

The chain was 4/10, 7/20, 8/20, then 14/20. No task, goal or predicate was changed (`.learnings/10-hard-questions.md`); the gains came from harness fixes (D36) and prompt v2 (D37), which was gated on the ScreenSpot dev split but motivated by three E2E failures (e2e-03, 13, 16). That is defensible, but a judge will still ask whether 70% holds on tasks you have never seen. Add 5 to 10 unseen tasks, or say plainly that none exist.

### 3.5 "Nobody else does this" is not true as stated

Microsoft Copilot Vision Highlights shows where to click inside a shared Windows app (blogs.windows.com, 2025-06-12). Gemini Live has camera overlays on Pixel 10 (2025-08). Digital adoption platforms (WalkMe, Whatfix, Pendo) do it inside their customers' own products. What none of them do, from the research in this review:

- redact on the device, with a mandatory human review, before anything is uploaded;
- work from a still screenshot of any app on any device, with no install, including someone else's device;
- produce an expiring, shareable guide for a remote relative;
- publish how often they are wrong.

That is the defensible novelty. Lead with it and name Copilot and Gemini yourself before a judge does.

### 3.6 The B2B widget thesis collides with incumbents

"ShowMe for your help centre" competes with DAPs that have DOM-level precision inside the company's own product, and companies do not need screenshot grounding for UI they own. Validation evidence: none (no interviews, LOIs or pilots, `.learnings/08`). Better buyers are organisations whose users are stuck in **other people's** apps: digital-inclusion charities and volunteer "digital buddy" schemes, bank vulnerable-customer and fraud teams, telcos and libraries running tech-help sessions. Keep the widget as a footnote.

### 3.7 SAM is a required Meta model with no proven benefit yet

IoU says SAM is slightly worse than the model's own box (0.514 vs 0.556); the human highlight ratings that D24 deferred the decision to are empty. Thirty minutes of rating `eval/e2e/highlight_ratings.csv` settles the claim either way. Either result is fine if reported; an unrated claim is not.

### 3.8 Smaller holes

- **Founder story is confirmed true (MEMORY D14) but unused.** The PRD §7 draft still says "founder to adjust the personal details", and the Q7 row in MEMORY still reads as open. Put the real details in the video opening and the submission text; it is the strongest emotional asset you have.
- **Official Rules not read** (MEMORY Q4). Submission format, video length and judging criteria are UNVERIFIED. Public listings say submissions close 2026-11-05 and judging runs Nov 5 to 9 (UNVERIFIED secondary source); your plan to submit Nov 4 18:00 London is safe either way.
- **Phone reality untested**: founder phone run and phone OCR timing are still open (PHASES Day 3, Day 4).
- **Redaction misses names, addresses and free text** (`.learnings/07`). The review screen is the safety net; nobody has timed it (R3 criterion: > 45 s average triggers "hide all text in boxes").
- **Production latency and cost** are only measured in evals (ScreenSpot-v2 held-out, prompt v1: p50 2.05 s, p95 2.91 s; $7.0427 / 1,172 calls ≈ $0.006 per step). Lighthouse 98/100 is recorded in MEMORY with no results file.
- **Missing required docs**: `REQUIREMENTS_STATUS.md`, `SECURITY.md`, `SUBMISSION.md`, `RUNBOOK.md`.

## 4. What it solves, and is it new

**Pain point:** help for a stuck person is either not grounded in their actual screen (articles, chatbots, phone calls), or it can see the screen but sends everything on it to a cloud (Copilot, Gemini, ChatGPT uploads). ShowMe is the only tool found that is both grounded and private before upload.

**Supporting evidence (cite carefully):**

- 65% of US adults 65+ get tech help from family at least sometimes (peer-reviewed, n = 1,173, Journal of Applied Gerontology, 2025; figure taken from the research summary, UNVERIFIED against the full paper).
- Tech-support scams are the most reported fraud type for people 60+ in FBI IC3 data; losses about $982M in 2024 (secondary source, UNVERIFIED; quote only from the IC3 PDF).
- Subscription cancellation is a live policy topic: the US FTC click-to-cancel rule was vacated in July 2025 and rulemaking restarted in 2026; UK DMCC subscription rules are expected in 2027 (dates UNVERIFIED).

**What is genuinely new, in one line for the pitch:** "The helper you can send a screenshot to, which never sees your private details and tells you when it does not know."

## 5. Will anyone actually use it

Yes, in three concrete situations, and not as a daily habit app:

1. **The remote helper** (strongest). A parent sends a screenshot; the helper makes a guide in a minute and sends the link. Frequency: a few times a month per family, high emotional value, natural word of mouth.
2. **The self-helper** who is confident enough to take screenshots but stuck on one screen: cancelling, privacy settings, government forms. Single use per problem.
3. **Volunteers and support staff** helping many people (library tech sessions, charities, bank branch staff): repeated use, the path to a paid product.

Who will **not** use it: the most anxious, least capable user operating it alone. Be explicit about that in the pitch; judges respect a scoped claim.

## 9. Where this goes after the hackathon

1. **Helper tool first** (consumer, free): guides made from screenshots, shared by link, with the parent opening them on a phone. Measure: guides created, guides opened, "it worked" taps.
2. **Organisations that help many people** (paid): digital-inclusion charities, libraries, bank and telco support for vulnerable customers. They need a dashboard of reusable guides and the same privacy guarantee. Validate with five conversations before writing code.
3. **Scam second opinion** (research): the `caution` and `refuse` states already exist (refusals 12/12 on held-out). A tested "Is it safe to tap this?" mode is a strong consumer hook, but only after a dedicated scam-screenshot eval.
4. **Long term, Meta-native surfaces**: the same grounding on a photo of a physical screen (ATM, TV, ticket machine) from a phone or Ray-Ban Meta glasses camera. This is a separate product with its own privacy model; do not claim it now.
